User flow

Two presses to the show

The front end is built around one promise: from the title screen, a fan is inside a live show in two presses. Everything else sits one level deep at most. There are only four places to go, and they never move.

The default path. "Tonight" always opens on whatever is live right now.

Artist

Studio works like a touring production office. The artist answers four questions, and every department (music, choreography, lip sync, lighting, video, FX, cameras, rights) does its job automatically. The artist only steps in where they want to, or where a department needs a decision.

1The actSolo, duo, boy/girl/mixed group, band, or DJ. Each member gets an avatar and a role.
→
2The musicDrop in songs. Stems, BPM and sections come back; vocal lines split across members.
→
3The movesGenre, "performs like" style, and moves from your own dance video, the style library, or both.
→
4The productionArena pop, club night, rock tour, festival rave or intimate. Pick a venue.

Rules

What keeps it simple as features pile up in Phases 2 to 4.

  1. Four top-level places only: Tonight, Shows, My tickets, Studio. New features live inside them.
  2. The primary action on every screen is pre-focused, so a controller user only presses A.
  3. B always goes back one level. Nothing is ever more than two levels deep.
  4. Live state is shown the same way everywhere: the red Live tally plus the watcher count.
  5. No settings or sign-in before the first show. Ask for an account only when it is needed (tickets, Studio).
  6. In a show the HUD shows four things and fades after 3 seconds without input.
Brand sheet · v0.3 street

Loud on the wall. Plain on the button.

StageVerse looks like a wheatpasted gig poster on a wet wall outside the venue, lit by the lasers leaking out of the doors. The posters, show art and title screen get to be loud, messy and street. The parts you press stay simple and readable, so the game is still easy to use.

Logo

The wordmark is set extra wide and heavy, with the V in red. The mark is that V drawn as a laser fan from one red emitter on the floor, which is how a real laser rig sits on a stage. Under the title screen the fan is live and follows the player's cursor.

Motion

The UI moves like a show, not like an app. Lasers are always running behind the menus at a low level and follow the cursor or right stick.

From prototype to UMG

How each piece of this prototype maps to Unreal. I'll write the full build plan as a doc once you approve the direction, then put the widgets in the repo.

Prototype pieceUnrealNotes
Screens and BackCommonUI activatable widget stackOne root layout with a menu layer and a modal layer. B and Esc pop the stack for free.
Controller prompts (A, B, ☰)CommonInputActionDataTable + CommonActionWidgetPrompts swap between Xbox, PlayStation, keyboard and touch automatically.
Colors, type, radiiUStageVerseStyle data asset + CommonUI text and button stylesOne place to change the gel palette. Fonts imported as composite font assets.
Show cards and Tonight heroCommonTileView fed by UShowListing objectsData comes from the Next.js API (/shows). Posters cached from the CDN.
Lasers and CO₂ behind the menusThe real arena level behind the UI: Niagara laser beams, exponential height fog with volumetric fog, CO₂ jets as Niagara smokeLasers are DMX fixtures driven by the same show-control system as the concert, so the title screen is a real idle cue. Cursor or right stick aims the fan; click or A on the fog fires a CO₂ cue. Real volumetrics on PC/console; iOS gets a baked fog card fallback.
